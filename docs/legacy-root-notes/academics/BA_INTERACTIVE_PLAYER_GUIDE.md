## Example: Playing "New Business Accounting Simulator"

```
╔═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╗
║  NEW BUSINESS ACCOUNTING SIMULATOR                            ║
║  Start a business and record all transactions                ║
╚═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════╝

SCENARIO:
You're starting a consulting business. Let's see how the 
accounting works as you make each business decision.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

QUESTION 1 OF 3

📌 You invest $50,000 of personal savings to start a consulting 
   business. After this transaction:
   
   Cash (Asset): $50,000
   Liabilities: $0
   
   What is Equity?

   A) $0
   B) $50,000  ✓ CORRECT
   C) $100,000
   D) -$50,000

✅ CORRECT!

💡 EXPLANATION:
   Your personal investment creates equity. The fundamental 
   equation works: Assets ($50K cash) = Liabilities ($0) + 
   Equity ($50K). Your investment IS the equity.

🎓 KEY LEARNING:
   Owner investment and equity creation

💰 POINTS EARNED: +15

[NEXT QUESTION →]

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

QUESTION 2 OF 3

📌 You borrow $30,000 from a bank for office equipment. Now:
   
   Assets: $80,000 (Cash $50K + Equipment $30K)
   Liabilities: $30,000 (Bank Loan)
   
   What is Equity?

   A) $50,000  ✓ CORRECT
   B) $110,000
   C) $30,000
   D) $80,000

✅ CORRECT!

💡 EXPLANATION:
   Equity = Assets - Liabilities = $80K - $30K = $50K.
   The loan increases assets but creates a liability. Your 
   equity stays the same because debt ≠ equity.

🎓 KEY LEARNING:
   Debt vs. equity financing

💰 POINTS EARNED: +15

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

QUESTION 3 OF 3

📌 You earn $15,000 in consulting revenue but collect only 
   $10,000 cash (rest owed by clients). How do you record this?

   A) Debit Cash $10K, Credit Revenue $10K
   B) Debit Cash $15K, Credit Revenue $15K
   C) Debit Cash $10K + Accounts Receivable $5K, Credit Revenue $15K ✓
   D) Debit Revenue $15K, Credit Cash $10K

✅ CORRECT!

💡 EXPLANATION:
   Full revenue is $15K ($10K cash + $5K owed). Accounts 
   Receivable tracks money customers still owe you. This is 
   accrual accounting—you record income when earned, not just 
   when cash arrives.

🎓 KEY LEARNING:
   Accrual accounting and receivables

💰 POINTS EARNED: +15

═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

🎉 GAME COMPLETE!

📊 RESULTS
  Correct Answers: 3/3
  Score: 100%
  Points Earned: +45

✅ PASSED (Required: 70%)

📚 KEY LEARNINGS FROM THIS GAME:
  ✓ How owner investment creates equity
  ✓ Difference between debt and equity financing
  ✓ Accrual accounting vs. cash accounting
  ✓ How accounts receivable works
  ✓ Building a real business's accounting
```

## Why This Approach Works

### ❌ Old Way (Text Only)
- Read chapter on accounting equation
- Read examples of journal entries
- Take a quiz with definitions
- **Result:** Players understand concepts but can't apply them

### ✅ New Way (Interactive + Practical)
- Play game where you build books for a startup
- Actually record transactions you encounter
- See real balance sheets and income statements
- Apply concepts to realistic business scenarios
- **Result:** Players understand AND can apply concepts

## Getting Started

Players discover interactive activities when they:

1. **Click a BA course** in the course catalog
2. **See the course modal** with mini-games listed
3. **Click "Play Game"** to start an interactive activity
4. **Complete the game** and earn points
5. **Return to course modal** to play next activity

No special setup needed—everything integrates seamlessly with the existing curriculum system!
