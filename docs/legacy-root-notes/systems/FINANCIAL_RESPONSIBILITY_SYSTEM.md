# 💸 Taxes & Real Financial Responsibility (SURPRISE MECHANIC)

## Overview

This is the **"surprise mechanic"** that teaches teens the harsh reality of adult finances:
- **Taxes take 20-35% of everything** (most teens have no idea)
- **Monthly expenses add up fast** ($1,000+ per month for living)
- **Unexpected expenses WILL happen** (medical, car, emergencies)
- **Debt is easy to fall into, hard to escape**
- **Financial stress directly affects health and mental health**

This system is designed to be **shocking**, **realistic**, and **educational**.

---

## The "Surprise" Elements

### 1. Tax Shock (The Big Revelation)

**What happens:**
- Player accepts job, expects $65,000/year salary
- First paycheck arrives
- **After taxes:** Only $51,616 take-home
- **20.6% disappeared** to federal tax, state tax, and FICA

**Teen reaction:** "WAIT, WHERE'S MY MONEY?!"

**Teaching moment:**
- Federal income tax: Progressive brackets (10-37%)
- State tax: 0-9% depending on state
- FICA (Social Security + Medicare): 7.65% of ALL earnings
- Standard deduction reduces taxable income
- Most young adults pay **25-35% effective tax rate**

**Story trigger:** [first_paycheck_shock]
> "Your paycheck arrives. You calculated $2,000 gross. But it's only $1,450 take-home. Federal tax, state tax, Social Security, Medicare... nearly 30% is gone. To taxes. You didn't even see it coming."

### 4. Financial Stress (Affects Health!)

**Stress calculation factors:**
- Debt ratio (debt / annual_income)
  - < 20% = healthy
  - 20-50% = concerned
  - 50-100% = stressed
  - > 100% = CRISIS (bankruptcy risk)
  
- Emergency fund months
  - 6+ months = excellent
  - 3-6 months = good
  - 1-3 months = okay
  - < 1 month = insufficient
  - 0 = none (stress +30)

**Health penalties (per semester):**
- Physical health: -0.3 per stress point
- Mental health: -1.0 per stress point
- Sleep quality affected

**Story triggers:**
- **Stress > 60:** "Rent Is Due Tomorrow" (cautionary)
- **Debt ratio > 0.8:** "The Debt Spiral" (cautionary)
- **Stress < 30 + emergency fund 3+ months:** "Financial Peace" (triumphant)

## Tax Mechanics (Realistic 2026 Federal Brackets)

**Progressive federal tax brackets (single filer):**
- 10% on income $0 - $11,000
- 12% on income $11,000 - $44,725
- 22% on income $44,725 - $95,375
- 24% on income $95,375 - $182,100
- 32% on income $182,100 - $231,250
- 35% on income $231,250 - $578,125
- 37% on income $578,125+

**Standard deduction:** $14,600 (reduces taxable income)

**State tax:** 0-9% depending on state (average 4%)

**FICA:** 7.65% flat on ALL gross income (no deductions)

**Effective rates by income:**
- $30,000: ~15% total tax
- $50,000: ~20% total tax
- $65,000: ~21% total tax
- $80,000: ~23% total tax
- $100,000: ~25% total tax

## Design Decisions

### Why Make Taxes a "Surprise"?

**Reality:** Most teens have never seen a paycheck after taxes. They calculate hourly wage × hours and expect that number. The first paycheck is ALWAYS a shock.

**Educational value:** Understanding that 25-35% of earnings disappear to taxes is critical financial literacy that most people don't learn until they're working.

**Balance:** System is realistic but not punishing—players still earn money, just less than they naively expected.

---

### Why Random Unexpected Expenses?

**Reality:** Life DOES throw unexpected expenses at you. Car breaks, you get sick, laptop dies, apartment floods. These aren't "if," they're "when."

**Educational value:** Teaches emergency fund necessity. Players who save for emergencies handle these events easily. Players who don't save go into debt.

**Balance:** Expenses are realistic ($400-$3,000 range), not catastrophic. Recovery is possible.

---

### Why Link Financial Stress to Health?

**Reality:** Financial stress is one of the leading causes of anxiety, depression, sleep problems, and relationship issues in young adults.

**Educational value:** Shows that money problems aren't just "numbers on a screen"—they affect your entire life, including physical and mental health.

**Balance:** Stress is manageable if player makes good decisions (emergency fund, paying off debt, budgeting).

## Teen Teaching Moments

### Moment 1: Tax Shock
**Setup:** Player accepts job at $65k/year, calculates "$5,417/month salary!"
**Reality:** First paycheck: $4,301/month take-home
**Lesson:** "Taxes are 20-35% of your earnings. Budget on TAKE-HOME, not gross."

### Moment 2: Emergency Happens
**Setup:** Player has $200 in emergency fund, $800 ER visit happens
**Reality:** Goes $600 into debt at 20% interest
**Lesson:** "Emergency fund should be 3-6 months of expenses. Always."

### Moment 3: Living Costs Add Up
**Setup:** Player moves off-campus, excited for "independence"
**Reality:** $1,910/month expenses ($22,920/year) = nearly half of $65k take-home
**Lesson:** "Living alone is EXPENSIVE. Shared housing saves thousands per year."

### Moment 4: Debt Spiral
**Setup:** Player accumulates $5,000 credit card debt at 22% interest
**Reality:** Minimum payment barely covers interest, takes 5+ years to pay off
**Lesson:** "Credit card debt is a trap. Pay it off aggressively or avoid it entirely."

### Moment 5: Financial Peace
**Setup:** Player builds $4,500 emergency fund, debt minimal, stress low
**Reality:** Unexpected $1,000 expense happens—easily covered, no stress
**Lesson:** "This is why emergency funds exist. Financial stability = peace of mind."
